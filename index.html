The principal ruling · the letter I

The Ministry finds that lowercase is a matter of nationality and of security, thus a matter of national security

Turkish has two letter I's, and they are different letters. Dotted i uppercases to dotted İ. Dotless ı uppercases to plain I. Lowercasing is therefore not a universal operation, and software that assumes it is has been failing in Turkey for thirty years, usually at the login screen.

A program lowercases what you typed, compares it against a stored value, and finds them different, because one machine was set to Turkish and the other was not. The comparison is correct. The strings are genuinely not the same. Nobody involved has made an error.

The consequence is not typographic

A system keeps you out of a reserved account by lowercasing what you typed and comparing it against a list. Lowercasing is a matter of nationality. Not yours: the machine's. The Ministry did not intend to acquire a national security brief. It is about to acquire one by deduction, which is the only way this Ministry acquires anything.

The reserved list is the same. The input is the same. The code is the same. Only the locale of the server differs, and on one of these two machines the guard does not hold. This is why every platform's guidance on the matter says the same thing: a case-insensitive comparison made for a security decision must be ordinal or invariant, never locale-aware. The check demonstrated above is the wrong one, written the way it is usually written.

The Ministry has no view on who ought to be admitted. It observes only that the answer presently depends on the nationality of the server, that the server was never asked to declare one, and that the person at the gate has no way of knowing which country they are being judged in.

The Ministry reached that conclusion by ordinary deduction, was startled by it, checked the working twice, and found the working sound. The two premises are demonstrated above, in the order they were established.

Schedule of standing contradictions

Jurisdictions in permanent, formal disagreement

Each of these is the correct order in the place that uses it. The Ministry has ruled in favour of all of them.

JurisdictionRulingOrder
Sweden Å, Ä and Ö are letters in their own right and come after Z. A Swedish telephone directory ends in a place an English one has never heard of. … x y z å ä ö
Germany Ä sorts as A in a dictionary and as AE in a telephone book. Both conventions are standardised, under the same standard, as parts one and two. ä = a, or ae
Estonia Z sits between S and T, which is where it has always been, and is a persistent surprise to every piece of software that assumed Z ends the alphabet. … s š z ž t …
Czechia CH is one letter and sorts after H, so chyba comes after hudba and well after cukr. … h ch i …
Spain, until 1994 CH and LL were letters, sorted after C and L. The academy abolished them as sorting units, and every book printed before the decision is still on the shelf, still ordered the old way. … c ch d … l ll m …
The machine, given no instructions Sorts by code point, so every capital letter precedes every lowercase one. Zebra arrives before apple. This is the only ordering on this page that no country uses and most software defaults to. A–Z then a–z
Schedule of closed matters

Matters the Ministry considers closed

Filed in the Ministry's own order, which is alphabetical. Four therefore precedes One, and Three precedes Two, because f comes before o and three comes before two. The Ministry is aware of how this looks and declines to file its rulings in an order it does not itself endorse.

Four

On the finality of rulings

All rulings of this Ministry are final within the jurisdiction that requested them and void everywhere else. This is not a limitation of the Ministry. It is the subject matter.

One

On the sorting of names by surname

The Ministry declines to define surname. Several jurisdictions place the family name first, several use no family name at all, and the Ministry has been asked to rule on which half of a name is the important half. It will not.

Three

On numbers appearing in text

Item 10 sorts before item 9 whenever the sort is alphabetical, which it usually is. The Ministry regards this as correct, widely understood, and the single most reported defect in its history. It notes that this ruling is numbered Three and, by alphabetical accident, is also filed third, the only point on this page where the two systems agree.

Two

On the position of the space

Whether New York precedes Newark depends on whether a space sorts before a letter. Both answers ship. The Ministry notes that the two orderings put the same two cities either side of each other and that nobody has ever noticed.
